HiNAS Control
Navigator Feedback

Insights on HiNAS Control from Avikus crew members who served as deck officers — viewed through three lenses: economy, safety, and reliability.

1
Same Fuel, Farther and More Precisely
•Automatic RPM optimization — Engine RPM is adjusted automatically to the optimized RPM plan, removing the burden of constant manual tuning.
•DFOC-based voyage — The ship sails to a target daily fuel-oil consumption, yet produces the optimal result for that same consumption — so the same fuel carries the vessel farther.
•Sub-waypoint control — Routes are split more finely than conventional waypoint plotting, giving precise per-segment speed and RPM recommendations and deeper fuel savings.
 
2
Prevent Accidents, Decide Sooner
•Collision avoidance (CAA / CAC) — Optimal avoidance and return routes are proposed and set in advance, speeding up decisions — most valuable for less-experienced officers on watch.
•Bridge decision support — In narrow channels and port approaches, AIS, ECDIS, radar and current data are integrated and ranked by risk, cutting cognitive overload.
•Proactive weather & Track Control — Real-time weather is combined with vessel-motion behaviour to flag dangerous areas early, while COG-based tracking holds the route against wind and current.
 
3
Control Stays With the Ship; Performance Is Proven
•Fail-safe architecture — If HiNAS Control fails, linked equipment (AP, BMS, ECDIS) keeps operating normally and the bridge can switch to manual instantly.
•Communication-loss resilience — Core functions are maintained even on ocean passages where satellite links are unstable.
•Verified performance — Functions such as TCS are demonstrated and validated during sea trials with crew present, reinforced by real-world cases from similar vessel types.

FAQ

Q. Is HiNAS Control recognized by classification societies?

A. HiNAS Control is a decision-support system designed to assist the watch officer. Responsibility for COLREG and other navigation rules always remains with the officer. As of v2.0.0, the system incorporates DNV Type Approval comments and has completed verification through sea trials.

Q. Does it really save fuel on long voyages?

A. Fuel savings of around 5% can typically be achieved, depending on vessel type and operating conditions. The system automatically adjusts engine RPM based on an optimized voyage plan and manages operations against a daily fuel oil consumption (DFOC) target.

Q. We mostly sail in open ocean — when does collision avoidance and route keeping help?

A. When course alteration is required to avoid a target, HiNAS Control proposes optimal avoidance and return routes in advance, and after passing it returns the vessel to the planned track — reducing watch workload on long ocean passages.

Q. What happens if the system fails or loses communication?

A. HiNAS Control is designed with a fail-safe architecture. In the event of a system failure, connected equipment (AP, BMS, ECDIS) continues to operate normally, and manual control can be resumed immediately. Core functions are maintained even during deep-sea passages where satellite communication may be unstable.
